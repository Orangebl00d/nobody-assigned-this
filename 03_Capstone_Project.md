# Capstone: Ship Something Real

## The requirements
Pick a **real problem** and solve it (for your family, school, a club, a local business, your community, or yourself), **or** ship a small project (a tool, website, app, video series, event, mini-business, or research piece). Either way:

1. **At least one real person outside your family** uses it, buys it, attends it, acts on it, or advises you on it.
2. **You use AI as a multiplier, with judgment.** Keep an AI Ledger showing what AI did, what you decided, and what you threw out.
3. **It ships by Demo Day.** Finished enough that a stranger could use it or act on it. It doesn't have to be perfect.

Time budget: about 4–6 hours across the two weeks, most of it inside the Session 4–7 challenges.

---

## Four sample ideas, from grounded to ambitious

### 1. Fix One Thing at School *(grounded, ~4 hours)*
Pick a problem from your Problem Log that lives at school: bikes locked to a fence, club sign-ups on paper that get lost, the lost-and-found nobody checks, a confusing course-selection process. Gather real evidence (count it, survey 20 students with a Google Form, photograph it). Use AI to research how other schools solved it and to draft options. Build a Level 4 proposal with a pilot plan, then deliver it to the person who can say yes.

**Outside person:** the decision-maker (principal, activities director, club advisor). **Shipped means:** they received it and gave you an answer, even "not yet."

### 2. Build a Tool People Use *(medium, ~5 hours)*
Use AI coding help to build a small web tool that at least 5 real people use. Examples: a converter that turns your school's rotating bell schedule into calendar events, a practice-log tracker for your team, a "what should I study tonight" planner that works from upcoming test dates, a sign-up page for your club. Host it free on something like GitHub Pages (a parent helps set up the account).

**Outside person:** your users. **Shipped means:** a live link, 5+ real users, and one change you made because of what they said.

### 3. Local Business Sprint *(medium-high, ~5 hours)*
Find a local small business with an obvious online gap: a pizzeria with an outdated menu online, a barber with no Google Business profile, a bakery whose website breaks on phones. Send the owner a Level 4 pitch (parent-reviewed) offering one specific improvement for free, as a learning project. Only start once they say yes. Then deliver it: a rewritten listing and FAQ, a simple one-page menu site, or a 20-customer survey with a one-page summary of what customers want.

**Outside person:** the owner. **Shipped means:** they approved it and used it.

### 4. Host the Thing *(ambitious, 6+ hours)*
Run a real event with 10+ attendees who aren't your family. Examples: a free 60-minute workshop for middle schoolers on using AI honestly for studying, held at the public library (with the librarian's permission); a neighborhood pickup tournament; a swap meet for used sports gear. Or, the research version: investigate a local question with real data and three interviews (How much food does the cafeteria throw away? Why does the only crosswalk near school have no signal?), write a two-page brief, and present it to someone who can act on it, such as the principal or the town council during public comment.

**Outside person:** attendees, interviewees, or the official you present to. **Shipped means:** the event happened, or the brief was delivered in person.

### 5. Bring Your Own
Anything that meets the three requirements. Run it by your mentor as a Level 4: problem, idea, why it's worth two weeks, what you need.

## Demo Format (5 minutes, then 5 minutes of Q&A)

| Segment | Time | Cover this |
|---|---|---|
| **The problem** | 0:45 | Who had it, with one real fact. Why you picked it. |
| **What I did** | 1:30 | Show it: the live thing, screenshots, the event photos, the brief. Walk through v0.1 → v0.2 → final. |
| **What AI did vs. what I decided** | 1:00 | Two or three examples of each from your AI Ledger. Include one AI output you rejected and why. |
| **Results** | 1:00 | What happened in the real world. Users, quotes, numbers, decisions, a yes, a no. |
| **What I'd do next** | 0:45 | The next step, and whether you're going to take it. |

No slides required. If you use them, five at most.

## Scoring Rubric

Score each criterion 1–4. The total is out of 24. This is for learning, not a grade: straight 3s is an excellent two weeks.

| Criterion | 1: Starting | 2: Developing | 3: Strong | 4: Exceptional |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem selection** | Vague or made-up problem; no evidence | A real problem, weak evidence or unclear who has it | Real problem, named people who have it, at least one piece of evidence | A problem others treated as weather; strong evidence; clear reason it was worth two weeks |
| **Initiative and resourcefulness** | Waited for instructions; stopped at the first obstacle | Took some initiative; needed prompting to keep going | Found their own way past most obstacles; asked for help specifically and early | Created opportunities nobody offered: new contacts, a side door, a bigger yes than expected |
| **AI with judgment and taste** | AI output passed along mostly unedited, or AI not used | AI used for drafts; some edits; decisions blurry | Clear AI Ledger; kept, cut, and changed AI work for stated reasons; facts checked | AI did work that would have taken days; student's decisions visibly shaped the result; rejected outputs explained well |
| **Shipping and real-world result** | Nothing shipped outside the family | Shipped, but no outside person engaged | At least one outside person used, bought, attended, or acted on it; feedback logged | Multiple outside users or a meaningful result (a decision, a customer, an event, a policy change) |
| **Handling obstacles** | Obstacles ended the project or went unmentioned | Obstacles noted; partial workaround | Named obstacles, used the unblock ladder, kept moving; followed up on silences and nos | Turned a no or a failure into a better version of the project |
| **Reflection and ownership** | Blames circumstances; vague reflection | Some honest reflection; limited ownership of misses | Owns misses specifically (four-part repair where needed); clear lessons | Candid, specific self-assessment; lessons already applied; a concrete next-30-day commitment |
