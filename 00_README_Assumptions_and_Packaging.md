# Nobody Assigned This: Read Me First

## Assumptions

- **One adult is lightly involved.** A parent or mentor joins for about 10 minutes of each session (mostly the discussion questions), reviews every outreach message before it's sent, and attends Demo Day. If the student runs a session solo, they answer two discussion questions in writing instead of out loud.
- **The pace is 8 sessions over 14 days**, roughly every other day, during a normal school stretch. Guided time is 230 minutes. Between-session work (challenges plus the capstone) adds about 5 to 7 hours spread over two weeks, and each quiz takes about 10 minutes at home.
- **The student has a general AI chat tool** (Claude, ChatGPT, or similar) that they use with a parent's knowledge and within the tool's age terms, plus a laptop and one running doc or notebook for the journal.
- **The quotes are used as supplied** in the brief, with attribution, and weren't independently re-checked. Shaan Puri's point is paraphrased, as instructed. No other quotes or stories about real people were added.
- **US context** (school email, town council, local businesses). Swap examples freely for another country.

## Recommended packaging

1. **Student Workbook** as one shared Google Doc: files 01 + 02 + 03 + 04 pasted in order. A shared doc lets the student write answers inline and lets the parent comment without taking over. Put the Reflection Journal at the very top so it's the first thing they open each day.
2. **One-page schedule** printed and posted somewhere visible. Checking off sessions is a small public commitment, which is on theme.
3. **Quiz sheets** printed. Pen on paper, no AI, 10 minutes.
4. **Parent Guide + answer keys** kept by the adult. The student can read the parent guide too; there's nothing secret in it, and knowing the adult has been told "don't rescue" changes how they ask for help.

All student-facing material is plain Markdown. It pastes cleanly into Google Docs (with the Markdown setting on), Notion, Obsidian, or any Markdown viewer.
